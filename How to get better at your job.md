# References

> [!QUOTE] Original Capture
> ### **1. Care More Than Average**
>
> The foundation of exceptional performance is genuine care. People who improve quickly are emotionally invested in doing great work, not merely completing assigned tasks. Caring creates the motivation to notice details, raise standards, and keep improving even when nobody is watching.
>
> ### **2. Get Reps**
>
> Skill improves through repeated practice. Mastery doesn’t come from consuming more advice or theory alone—it comes from doing the work over and over. Repetition builds intuition, pattern recognition, and confidence in real situations.
>
> ### **3. Seek Discomfort**
>
> Growth happens at the edge of competence. If work feels too comfortable, improvement slows because you are relying on existing strengths instead of developing new ones. Taking on difficult tasks, hard conversations, or unfamiliar challenges accelerates learning.
>
> ### **4. Ask for Feedback**
>
> Improvement requires external perspective. Blind spots are difficult to identify alone, so feedback from managers, peers, or mentors helps reveal weaknesses and opportunities for growth. The best performers actively seek criticism instead of waiting for formal reviews.
>
> ### **5. Study People Better Than You**
>
> Accelerated learning often comes from observing excellence. Watching how high performers think, communicate, prioritize, and solve problems helps you internalize stronger standards and better mental models.
>
> ### **6. Reflect on Your Work**
>
> Experience alone doesn’t guarantee progress—reflection turns experience into learning. Regularly reviewing what worked, what failed, and why helps refine judgment and prevents repeated mistakes.
>
> ### **7. Play the Long Game**
>
> Getting great at your job is a compounding process. Meaningful growth rarely comes from dramatic short-term leaps; it comes from small improvements sustained over years. Consistency matters more than intensity.
>
> The article’s core message is that becoming exceptional at work is not mysterious: **care deeply, practice deliberately, seek challenge, learn from feedback, and keep compounding improvements over time**.
