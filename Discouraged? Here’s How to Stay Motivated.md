> # Discouraged? Here’s How to Stay Motivated
>
> Setbacks can severely damage motivation because failure feels much more painful than progress feels rewarding. When repeated attempts fail, people may lose confidence, avoid further effort, and create a self-fulfilling cycle of failure.
>
> However, persistence is not always the right choice. Giving up can be rational when other opportunities are better, when the chances of success are too low, or when the required effort is too great. Before trying to rebuild motivation, objectively assess whether continuing remains the best option by considering:
>
> - The likelihood of succeeding with additional effort.
> - The time and energy required.
> - The opportunities available if you choose a different path.
>
> If continuing is still worthwhile, motivation is best rebuilt through a clear, credible plan—not temporary hype, affirmations, or positive thinking. The plan should consist of realistic, doable steps that logically lead toward the desired outcome.
>
> A strong plan does not need to guarantee success. It may be worthwhile if the probability of success is acceptable or if the attempt provides valuable information. For example, repeated job-application failures may reveal that the résumé or strategy needs improvement, making further experimentation useful.
>
> When no convincing plan exists, the next step is to research more or adopt an exploratory mindset. Experiment with different approaches, gather information, and look for a path that appears likely to work.
>
> Ultimately, persistence depends less on willpower than on rationally evaluating the situation and creating a strategy you genuinely believe in.
