# References

> [!QUOTE] Original Capture
> * Reading “better” books does not automatically make you smarter. Sophisticated books can also make you confidently wrong.
>
> * Many difficult or classic books assume prior background knowledge. Without foundations, readers often misunderstand or oversimplify them.
>
> * The best book for you is not necessarily the most prestigious one — it’s the one that stretches your understanding without overwhelming you.
>
> * Textbooks and introductory survey books are underrated because they are usually designed around consensus knowledge instead of persuasion or novelty.
>
> * Popular nonfiction often optimizes for compelling narratives and bold claims rather than balanced understanding.
>
> * Use tools like ChatGPT or Wikipedia to quickly understand:
>
>   * whether an idea is mainstream or fringe
>   * what criticisms exist
>   * where an author sits in broader intellectual debates
>
> * Read multiple books on the same topic instead of relying on one author. This prevents a single worldview from dominating your thinking.
>
> * Actively seek rebuttals and opposing viewpoints to stress-test ideas.
>
> * Motivation matters:
>
>   * Reading to genuinely understand leads to growth.
>   * Reading to feel smart, join a tribe, or confirm beliefs often increases bias.
>
> * Core takeaway:
>   Intellectual growth comes less from consuming prestigious books and more from building foundations, comparing perspectives, and staying open to revising your beliefs.
