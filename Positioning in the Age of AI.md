> ### **1. A Point of View Is No Longer Optional**
>
> In rapidly changing AI markets, customers expect vendors to do more than describe features—they expect them to explain **where the market is going**. Strong positioning now requires a clear point of view about industry change, what will matter in the future, and how customers should prepare. Companies that lack this perspective risk appearing interchangeable. ([April Dunford's Substack][1])
>
> ### **2. Position Against Your Real Competition (While Preparing for Tomorrow)**
>
> Positioning should reflect **the competitive battle you're fighting today**, not the one you hope to fight years from now. While product teams should build for the future, marketing and sales must help customers understand why the current product is the best choice today. Positioning too far ahead of customer reality creates confusion instead of differentiation. ([April Dunford's Substack][1])
>
> ### **3. Sell the Future, Connect It to the Present**
>
> Buyers are excited by AI's potential but also worried about investing in technology that could quickly become obsolete. Companies need an ambitious vision of the future **and** a credible roadmap that explains how customers can move from today's workflows to tomorrow's AI-powered capabilities. Vision without a practical transition plan creates uncertainty. ([April Dunford's Substack][1])
>
> ### **4. Understand How Customers Actually Think**
>
> AI hype can distort a company's understanding of the market. Rather than assuming customer priorities, businesses should maintain continuous feedback through customer interviews, advisory boards, executive sponsorships, and win/loss analysis. Positioning should be grounded in **actual customer behavior**, not industry excitement or internal assumptions. ([April Dunford's Substack][1])
>
> ### **The Core Message**
>
> AI has made positioning more challenging because technology changes faster than customer adoption. The winning companies will not simply talk the most about AI—they will **provide a compelling perspective on the future, position for customers' current reality, connect today's problems to tomorrow's vision, and stay deeply grounded in how customers actually make decisions**. ([April Dunford's Substack][1])
