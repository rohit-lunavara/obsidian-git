> [!QUOTE] Original Capture
> ### **The Core Idea**
>
> The bottleneck in agentic software development is no longer AI throughput—it is **human cognitive bandwidth**. Running more AI agents in parallel increases output, but it also increases the mental load required to supervise, verify, and coordinate them.
>
> ### **What Parallel Agents Actually Ask of You**
>
> Managing multiple agents is fundamentally different from working with one. Instead of tracking a single stream of work, you're maintaining multiple mental models simultaneously—each with its own context, assumptions, progress, and potential failure modes. This transforms the developer's role from collaborator to manager. ([Addy Osmani][1])
>
> ### **The Ambient Anxiety Tax**
>
> Much of the cognitive cost comes from **background vigilance** rather than active work. Even when you're focused on one agent, part of your attention is occupied wondering whether another agent has gone off track. This constant low-level monitoring quietly drains mental energy throughout the day. ([Addy Osmani][1])
>
> ### **Comprehension Debt Compounds**
>
> AI can generate work faster than humans can understand it. With one agent, this "comprehension debt" is manageable. With several agents running concurrently, the debt accumulates across multiple threads, making review increasingly difficult and raising the likelihood of missing subtle errors. ([Addy Osmani][1])
>
> ### **Context Switching Is More Expensive Than It Looks**
>
> Every switch between agents requires rebuilding the mental model for that task: what decisions were made, what constraints exist, and what still needs verification. The real cost isn't the switch itself but the recovery time needed to regain context. Frequent switching prevents deep focus and accelerates mental fatigue. ([Addy Osmani][1])
>
> ### **Find Your Personal Parallel Limit**
>
> There is no universal optimal number of concurrent agents. Each person has a different cognitive ceiling, influenced by task complexity, familiarity, and experience. Beyond that limit, additional agents reduce overall effectiveness because supervision quality deteriorates faster than throughput increases. ([Addy Osmani][1])
>
> ### **Optimize for Sustainable Throughput**
>
> The objective is not maximizing the number of agents but maximizing **high-quality output over time**. Fewer well-managed agents often outperform a larger swarm that overwhelms the human reviewer. Treat your own attention as the scarcest resource in the system and design workflows that preserve it. ([Addy Osmani][1])
