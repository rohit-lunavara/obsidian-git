> Typography hierarchy is the deliberate use of differences in size, weight, spacing, contrast, and placement to guide users through an interface. Good hierarchy reduces cognitive load by making it immediately clear what deserves attention first, second, and third.
> 
> Effective UI typography improves usability by helping users scan faster, understand structure, and make decisions with less effort. Without hierarchy, all text competes for attention, creating confusion and slowing comprehension.
> 
> Strong hierarchy relies on consistent distinctions between text roles—such as headings, subheadings, body text, captions, and metadata—so users can quickly recognize patterns across screens.
> 
> Key techniques include:
> 
> Using larger or bolder text for primary information
> Increasing whitespace and spacing to separate sections
> Applying contrast and color to emphasize importance
> Maintaining consistent typographic scales across the product
> 
> Good typography is not about adding visual flair; it is about clarity and information prioritization. The goal is to create interfaces where users instinctively know where to look and what to do next, making the experience feel intuitive and effortless.
